# GOBLIN-Spatial scenario architecture

## Core principle

GOBLIN determines the national pathway. GOBLIN-Spatial does not construct a future ED livestock population from scratch. It starts from the selected validated ED baseline (2020 or 2025), subtracts the national change required by GOBLIN from animals already present, propagates the associated herd response through ED and within-county livestock geography, and then evaluates livestock pressure, production exposure, spared grassland and downstream land-use opportunity.

For livestock category k at milestone t:

`IncrementalNationalReduction(k,t) = NationalState(k,t-1) - GOBLINTarget(k,t)`

At ED level:

`Scenario(e,k,t) = PreviousState(e,k,t-1) - IncrementalReduction(e,k,t)`

and relative to the selected baseline:

`CumulativeReduction(e,k,t) = Baseline(e,k) - Scenario(e,k,t)`

The pathway is therefore baseline-relative and cumulative, never a fresh redistribution of a future national total.

## National milestones

Direct GOBLIN values for 2030, 2040 and 2050 are preferred. If only a 2050 endpoint is available, intermediate values may be generated by an explicitly declared interpolation rule. Linear interpolation is the transparent default fallback, not a prediction of the real transition path.

For a contraction pathway, each ED state must be non-increasing through time unless an explicit expansion process is introduced:

`Baseline >= 2030 >= 2040 >= 2050`

The scenario experiment has three independent dimensions: GOBLIN/net-zero pathway, spatial allocation rule and milestone year. The GOBLIN pathway determines the national scale and timing of adjustment. The allocation rule determines where the fixed adjustment occurs.

## Adult allocation rules

At each milestone, the model allocates only the additional dairy-cow, suckler-cow and sheep reduction required since the preceding state.

- PRORATA: approximately equal proportional cuts across the existing footprint.
- DAIRY_PROTECTION: EDs with larger baseline dairy herds receive smaller proportional dairy cuts.
- PRODUCTIVITY_PROTECTION: EDs with higher pre-scenario productivity receive stronger protection.
- VULNERABILITY_PROTECTION: EDs with higher pre-scenario vulnerability receive stronger protection.
- HYBRID_BALANCED: configurable combination of proportionality, productivity, dairy concentration and vulnerability.
- SCORE_WEIGHTED: generic pre-scenario protection score for study-specific rules.

Protection indicators are fixed from the selected baseline/pre-scenario state to avoid circular allocation. No rule may change the national GOBLIN target.

## Adult and cattle cohorts are one herd adjustment

Adult and follower changes belong to the same milestone herd state. For an active breeding ED, the local marginal cohort signal is:

`cohort_previous * (incremental_adult_reduction / adult_previous)`

which is algebraically equivalent to:

`incremental_adult_reduction * (cohort_previous / adult_previous)`

Thus dairy reductions propagate first through the ED's DxD/DxB structure, suckler reductions through BxB, and bulls through the relevant adult-cow structure. ED heterogeneity is retained rather than replacing all EDs with one national follower ratio.

GOBLIN/COHORTS remains authoritative for the national 21-cattle-cohort endpoint at every milestone. ED relationships determine spatial incidence, not national biology.

## ED first, county second

Some EDs are receiver, rearing or finishing locations: they may contain substantial young cattle despite having few or no corresponding adult cows. These EDs must still respond when the breeding population supplying them contracts.

The spatial hierarchy is:

`ED direct response -> same-county receiver/rearing/finishing response -> sparse orphan fallback -> exact national GOBLIN/COHORTS closure`

An active breeding ED uses its own realised adult reduction rate. A receiver ED with no corresponding adults uses the reduction rate of relevant breeding adults in the same county. National fallback is used only when the county itself has no corresponding breeding stock for a cohort already present there. National closure must not be interpreted as unconstrained livestock movement between distant counties.

## Cumulative 21-cattle and 10-sheep pathways

For cattle, every milestone carries exact GOBLIN/COHORTS national targets for all 21 cohorts and produces, by ED:

- `BASE_COHORT_*`
- `PREVIOUS_COHORT_*`
- `INCREMENTAL_REDUCTION_COHORT_*`
- `CUMULATIVE_REDUCTION_COHORT_*`
- `SCENARIO_COHORT_*`

For sheep, the exact ED total-sheep pathway is disaggregated through the existing ten GOBLIN sheep cohorts while preserving each ED's lowland/upland and age structure as closely as integer accounting permits. If independent national ten-cohort sheep controls are supplied by a future GOBLIN run, they should be added as reconciliation controls rather than inferred by GOBLIN-Spatial.

The combined output is a cumulative 31-cohort livestock state for every ED and milestone.

## LSU is a diagnostic, not the spared-land equation

Scenario LSU can be calculated from the reconciled livestock state for reporting, stocking-pressure diagnostics and comparison with official livestock-unit statistics. It is useful, but it is not the primary GOBLIN spared-area calculation.

The upstream GOBLIN grassland-production model determines land requirement from pasture dry-matter demand, grass yield and utilisation. GOBLIN-Spatial therefore follows that feed-balance structure rather than assuming that a percentage reduction in LSU automatically releases the same percentage of grassland.

## GOBLIN-style pasture dry-matter accounting

For each ED e and state t, livestock pasture demand is calculated from the full cattle and sheep cohort population:

`PastureDM(e,t) = sum_k Population(e,k,t) * PastureDMPerHead(k,t)`

The pasture-DM-per-head coefficients should come from the same GOBLIN cattle/sheep feed logic used by `grassland_production`. They are external scientific controls and are not guessed by GOBLIN-Spatial.

The upstream GOBLIN grassland calculation is conceptually:

`RequiredGrassland = PastureDMRequirement / WeightedGrassYield / UtilisationRate`

GOBLIN-Spatial preserves the validated ED grassland baseline by calibrating an effective baseline pasture supply for each ED:

`BaselineEffectiveSupply(e) = BaselinePastureDM(e) / BaselineAgriculturalGrassland(e)`

This makes the validated ED baseline an exact fixed point of the feed-balance calculation without pretending that a national-average yield is directly observed in every ED.

Future yield and utilisation changes are introduced explicitly as a pasture-supply multiplier:

`ScenarioEffectiveSupply(e,t) = BaselineEffectiveSupply(e) * SupplyMultiplier(e,t)`

where 1.0 means unchanged effective yield/utilisation and values above 1 represent an explicitly specified improvement.

Then:

`ScenarioRequiredGrassland(e,t) = ScenarioPastureDM(e,t) / ScenarioEffectiveSupply(e,t)`

and the signed grassland balance is:

`SignedGrasslandBalance(e,t) = BaselineAgriculturalGrassland(e) - ScenarioRequiredGrassland(e,t)`

Positive spared area is:

`PotentialSparedGrassland(e,t) = max(0, SignedGrasslandBalance(e,t))`

If the balance is negative, the model reports:

`AdditionalGrasslandRequired(e,t) = max(0, -SignedGrasslandBalance(e,t))`

rather than hiding an infeasible or expansionary feed requirement.

The land base is the validated agricultural `ALL_GRASSLAND` from GOBLIN-Spatial, not generic physical grass cover.

## Interpretation of spared grassland

`POTENTIAL_SPARED_GRASSLAND_HA` is the ED-level counterpart of the GOBLIN destocked/spared grassland concept: baseline grassland requirement minus scenario grassland requirement under the stated animal, feed, yield and utilisation assumptions.

It is not automatically abandoned land, forestry, nature restoration, rewetting, cropland or bioenergy land. Three concepts remain separate:

`PotentialRelease != Opportunity != RealisedConversion`

Spared-area origin can later be attributed to livestock systems/cohorts using dry-matter reduction contributions, consistent with the upstream GOBLIN approach.

## Standard Output exposure

Standard Output is evaluated after the scenario livestock state has been established:

`BaselineSO(e) = sum_k BaselineActivity(e,k) * SOCoefficient(k)`

`ScenarioSO(e,t) = sum_k ScenarioActivity(e,k,t) * SOCoefficient(k)`

`SOLoss(e,t) = BaselineSO(e) - ScenarioSO(e,t)`

SO is production-value exposure, not farm income, profit or welfare. Income, labour and capital exposure from FADN/IFS-type evidence remain separate modules, and mapped regional/system coefficients must be labelled as mapped rather than directly observed ED economics.

## Opportunity and realised land allocation

Only after spared grassland has been identified should soil, land capability, organic-soil status, drainage and other spatial constraints be used to screen potential alternative uses. Candidate opportunity layers include nature/restoration, forestry, rewetting, cropland, AD grass and retained low-intensity grassland.

Opportunity layers may overlap initially; they are not additive hectares until a mutually exclusive land-allocation rule is applied.

Where GOBLIN already specifies national afforestation, rewetting, AD-grass, cropland or other deployment targets, GOBLIN-Spatial should spatialise those national controls across eligible released land rather than inventing new national totals. If no deployment target is supplied, GOBLIN-Spatial reports opportunity only.

## Full workflow

`GOBLIN/net-zero definition`

`-> national 2030/2040/2050 livestock pathway`

`-> selected 2020 or 2025 ED baseline`

`-> spatial allocation rule`

`-> incremental ED adult reduction`

`-> simultaneous ED adult/cohort response`

`-> same-county receiver/rearing/finishing response`

`-> exact national 21-cattle cohort closure + 10-sheep ED closure`

`-> complete 31-cohort ED livestock state`

`-> LSU diagnostic`

`-> pasture dry-matter requirement`

`-> effective grass yield/utilisation`

`-> scenario grassland requirement`

`-> potential spared grassland`

`-> Standard Output exposure`

`-> soil/land-capability opportunity screen`

`-> spatialisation of any GOBLIN national land-use targets`

`-> ED, county, regional and national transition outputs`

The central analytical distinction remains unchanged: GOBLIN determines how much national adjustment is required, while the allocation rule determines where that adjustment occurs. The resulting 31-cohort spatial herd determines production exposure and the geography of grassland release, and only then do land suitability and national land-use targets determine possible future use.
